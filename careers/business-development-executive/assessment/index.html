Your Objective

Think like a Business Development Executive.

openEd Labs is expanding its footprint among academic institutions, universities, colleges, and training organizations.

Your objective is to identify a high-value prospect, understand who can influence a potential engagement, and create a credible path toward an initial discovery conversation.

The outcome we want

We are not looking for a generic sales pitch. We want to see whether you can research an account, understand its potential needs, personalize your communication, and create enough relevance to earn a 15-minute conversation.

  • Research before reaching out.
  • Identify the right decision-makers.
  • Lead with business value.
  • Handle resistance without being pushy.
  • Ask for a specific next step.
The Assessment

Your Business Development Challenge

Select one real academic institution or training provider that you believe could be a strong potential client for openEd Labs. Then complete all five steps below.

01

Target Account & Persona Identification

Select one real academic institution or training provider.

  • Institution name
  • Official website link
  • Two relevant decision-makers
  • Full name and job title
  • LinkedIn URL
  • Calculated / inferred email pattern
  • Two sentences explaining why the institution is a strong fit for openEd Labs
02

Initial Cold Outreach

Write the actual cold email you would send to one of your selected decision-makers.

  • Include a compelling subject line
  • Personalize the message
  • Focus on relevance and value
  • Avoid a generic feature dump
  • End with a clear call-to-action
Maximum 150 words
03

Handling the Resistance

Assume the prospect responds:

“We already use an existing platform / in-house solution and aren't looking to add or switch tools right now.”

Write your exact email response.

  • Do not argue with the prospect
  • Reframe openEd Labs as complementary
  • Demonstrate understanding of their situation
  • Create a reason to continue the conversation
  • Ask for a 15-minute introductory call
04

60-Second Elevator Pitch

Pitch openEd Labs directly to the decision-maker as if you were speaking to them on a discovery call or introductory video.

  • Record a 60–90 second Loom video
  • OR provide a verbal script of approximately 100 words
  • Make it conversational and customer-centric
  • Explain why a conversation is worth their time
  • Finish with a meeting request
05

Research Quality

Your research should demonstrate that you selected the account and personas intentionally rather than randomly.

  • Use reliable public sources
  • Verify the institution's current website
  • Verify decision-maker roles
  • Use LinkedIn for professional verification
  • Clearly distinguish verified information from an inferred email pattern
06

Think Beyond the First Email

Your submission should show that you understand business development as a conversation rather than simply sending an email.

  • Identify a meaningful institutional problem
  • Connect that problem to openEd Labs
  • Keep the prospect's priorities central
  • Handle objections professionally
  • Create a logical next step

Sales Execution

Show us how you would start the conversation.

The purpose of the exercise is not to sell everything openEd Labs does. The purpose is to demonstrate that you can identify a relevant business opportunity and earn the right to have a deeper conversation.

Your Cold Email

Write the exact message you would send. Keep it concise, specific to the institution, and focused on a relevant opportunity rather than listing every openEd Labs service.

Maximum 150 words

Your Objection Response

The prospect already has a platform or internal solution. Your response should position openEd Labs as an additional capability or potential partner, not force the prospect into a platform replacement.

Your CTA

Your objective is to secure a short introductory conversation. Ask for a specific 15-minute meeting rather than an open-ended “let me know if you're interested.”

Your Pitch

Explain openEd Labs naturally, as if you were speaking directly to your selected decision-maker. Focus on their potential needs and why a conversation could be worthwhile.
